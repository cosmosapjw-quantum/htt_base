## 2. Mathematical Formulation & Covariant Multipole Expansions

### 2.1 Spacetime Decomposition & Cosmographic Parameters
Following covariant cosmography (Heinesen 2021, Maartens et al. 2024), we parameterize the observer-frame null geodesic bundle. Let $\boldsymbol{n}$ be the unit spatial direction vector ($n^i n^i = 1$). The local directional expansion rate is:
$$
\mathcal{H}_u(\boldsymbol{n}) = H \left( 1 + \boldsymbol{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{n} + \boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n} \right),
$$
where:
$$
\boldsymbol{d} = -\frac{\boldsymbol{A}}{cH} = \frac{\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}}{cH}, \qquad \boldsymbol{S} = \frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{H}, \qquad \operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{S}) = 0.
$$
Here $\boldsymbol{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ represents dimensionless observer acceleration/dipole, and $\boldsymbol{S} \in \operatorname{STF}_2(\mathbb{R}^3)$ represents dimensionless quadrupole shear ($\dim = 5$).

### 2.2 Nonlinear Magnitude Field & Octupole Generation
The intercept-subtracted apparent magnitude field $m(\boldsymbol{n})$ observed at low redshift ($z \ll 1$) is:
$$

$$
Taylor-expanding the logarithm for small deviations $\sup_{\boldsymbol{n}} |\boldsymbol{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{n} + \boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n}| \le \epsilon < 1$:
$$
f(\boldsymbol{n}) = -\kappa \left[ (\boldsymbol{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{n} + \boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n}) - \frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{n} + \boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n})^2 + \frac{1}{3}(\boldsymbol{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{n} + \boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n})^3 - \dots \right].
$$
Projecting onto the irreducible 7-dimensional symmetric trace-free octupole basis $\operatorname{STF}_3(\mathbb{R}^3)$:
$$
\boldsymbol{O} = \kappa \boldsymbol{L}_S \boldsymbol{d} + \boldsymbol{R}_3,
$$
where the bilinear coupling operator $\boldsymbol{L}_S: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \operatorname{STF}_3(\mathbb{R}^3)$ is:
$$
(\boldsymbol{L}_S \boldsymbol{d})_{ijk} = \operatorname{STF}\left( d_{(i} S_{jk)} \right) = \frac{1}{3}\left( d_i S_{jk} + d_j S_{ki} + d_k S_{ij} \right) - \frac{2}{15}\left( \delta_{ij} S_{kl} d_l + \delta_{jk} S_{il} d_l + \delta_{ki} S_{jl} d_l \right).
$$
The normal Gram matrix of this coupling satisfies the exact identity:
$$
\boldsymbol{L}_S^T \boldsymbol{L}_S = \frac{\boldsymbol{S}:\boldsymbol{S}}{3}\boldsymbol{I}_3 + \frac{2}{5}\boldsymbol{S}^2 = \frac{\|\boldsymbol{S}\|_F^2}{3}\boldsymbol{I}_3 + \frac{2}{5}\boldsymbol{S}^2.
$$
The residual truncation error $\boldsymbol{R}_3$ is bounded deterministically by:
$$
\|\boldsymbol{R}_3\|_F \le \sqrt{\frac{35}{2}} \frac{\kappa \epsilon^3}{3(1-\epsilon)}.
$$

### 2.3 The 4-Dimensional Orthogonal Subspace
Because $\dim(\mathbb{R}^3) = 3$ and $\dim(\operatorname{STF}_3) = 7$, for any fixed non-zero shear $\boldsymbol{S} \ne \boldsymbol{0}$, the image $\operatorname{Im}(\boldsymbol{L}_S)$ is at most 3-dimensional.
- The orthogonal projection onto the kernel/out-of-response subspace is:
  $$
  \boldsymbol{P}_\perp = \boldsymbol{I}_7 - \boldsymbol{L}_S (\boldsymbol{L}_S^T \boldsymbol{L}_S)^{-1} \boldsymbol{L}_S^T, \qquad \operatorname{rank}(\boldsymbol{P}_\perp) = 4.

- The perpendicular octupole component $\boldsymbol{O}_\perp = \boldsymbol{P}_\perp \boldsymbol{O}$ cannot be generated by kinematic dipole–shear coupling of the local expansion.
