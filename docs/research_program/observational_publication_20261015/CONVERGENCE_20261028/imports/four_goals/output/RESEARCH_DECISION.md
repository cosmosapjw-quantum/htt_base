## Matrix CBL: a useful research route, not yet a fast-track novelty claim

The scalar CBL test, distance-opacity degeneracy and joint curvature-opacity analyses already exist. MF02 itself contains the scalar opacity and Jordan matrix identities. The matrix identity follows a short Leibniz calculation; its correctness alone is insufficient for an independent methods-paper advantage.

The stronger candidate is an *opacity-qualified identified-region analysis*: first-derivative curvature constancy, directional local-Hubble intervals, a common calibration/opacity event, and the exact partial-distance interval under bounded transmission. Polarimetric shape can add information only after source and detector calibration; it does not identify grey loss by itself. A matched scalar opacity-aware analysis is the required baseline. Current standard-candle data do not automatically satisfy complete quantum-process-tomography source spanning.

Wang et al. (2017), arXiv:1709.07258, already infer curvature and opacity jointly. Clarkson–Bassett–Lu (2008) already gives the lower-derivative curvature-constancy route. The contribution must therefore be the newly qualified measurement/uncertainty connection or demonstrated additional channel information, with the original sources acknowledged.
