Sharp quadrupole–octupole chirality and the nonlinear local distance response

                                       Anonymous review draft

         An angular observable can contain an octupole even when its underlying local rate contains
       only a dipole and a quadrupole. We show how the logarithm of the directional distance law
       generates this coupling, and identify the tensor operator that controls its geometry. For real
       symmetric trace-free tensors Q and O of ranks two and three, the contraction v = O : Q fills
       a solid ellipsoid at fixed octupole norm. Its minimum-norm lift yields the attained maximum
       of the pseudoscalar χ = det[v, Qv, Q2 v], and optimization over quadrupole shape gives the
                                √
       sharp inequality |χ| ≤ (4 2/135)∥Q∥6F ∥O∥3F . We give its equality cases, both signs, and
       the complete fixed-norm fibres, including zero and repeated-spectrum sectors. The same lift
       generates the leading acceleration–shear contribution to the local magnitude octupole. A
       finite logarithmic remainder bounds its four-component residual and the error of a construc-
       tive acceleration inverse. An explicit positive local-response family approaches both sharp
       chirality endpoints as its amplitude tends to zero. The complete ideal magnitude field also
       determines the dimensionless dipole and shear independently of an additive intercept. Re-
       tained spherical quadratures illustrate the quadratic coupled response, its cubic pure-dipole
       limit and the analytic remainder envelope.



                                        I.   INTRODUCTION


   Directional observations retain more information than their multipole powers. A quadrupole
supplies principal directions and eigenvalue shape; an octupole carries a relative orientation and
components that cannot be reconstructed from one preferred axis. Their joint geometry can there-
fore matter even when their scalar powers are fixed. Multipole-vector constructions provide a
familiar representation of this information [1], while harmonic-tensor invariant theory and tensor-
moment pseudoscalars supply a broader mathematical setting [2–4].
   We study the contraction of an octupole with a quadrupole. Repeated application of the
quadrupole to this vector gives three vectors whose oriented volume measures one aspect of the
pair’s handedness. The resulting quantity has a useful optimization problem: at prescribed tensor
norms, which contractions and oriented volumes are actually attained? Solving that problem gives
a bounded shape coordinate, together with the tensor components it leaves unresolved.
   There is a local optical use of the same construction. The directional redshift–distance slope of a
smooth congruence contains expansion, acceleration and shear. The logarithm entering a distance
modulus mixes their angular components. Its acceleration–shear product generates an octupole
                                                                                                                  2


whose three-dimensional image is exactly the adjoint image of the algebraic contraction. We derive
this relation in one source and observer congruence, following the local distance expansion of Kris-
tian and Sachs and Heinesen [5, 6]. The response considered here is the nonlinear conversion of a
distance to a magnitude. Changing the physical observer introduces the additional transformations
studied in covariant cosmography [7].
   The argument proceeds from the exact tensor image to its sharp oriented-volume bound, and
then to the optical response and inverse. The geometric statements are deterministic. The numerical
illustration evaluates a specified local response and supplies no sampling distribution for an observed
sky.

                                       VII.   DISCUSSION


   The contraction O : Q reduces seven octupole components to three, but its lost information
has a simple exact form. A four-dimensional affine kernel remains, and a norm constraint turns its
interior fibres into three-spheres. The ellipsoid boundary selects a unique minimum-norm tensor.
Balancing the three normalized contraction components then gives the fixed-quadrupole chirality
maximum; a zero cubic trace of the quadrupole gives the sharp global constant.

   The local distance response provides an interpretation of that boundary lift. Acceleration and
shear enter the directional rate at first order, and their product generates a constrained octupole
after the logarithmic magnitude conversion. Its complementary four components provide a residual
morphology test, with a finite angular bound and a constructive inverse. The complete ideal field
supplies an intercept-independent inverse, while an explicit small-amplitude family approaches both
algebraic chirality endpoints.

   These results organize amplitude, shape and response before a statistical law is introduced. An
observational use can pass a joint tensor region through the same maps, with its finite-redshift,
frame and calibration response retained. That preserves the information required to ask whether a
