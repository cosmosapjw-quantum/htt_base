## 2. Remote electron-frame dipole of the same compensated mode

Retain the earlier R63 assumptions: signature (-,+,+,+), sourceward sky directions, physical velocities divided by c, total-energy-rest normal N, separately conserved homogeneous dust and one radiation velocity on a finite post-decoupling interval. Let

    beta_m(z)=(1+z)b,  beta_r(z)=-R b,
    R=3epsilon_m0/(4epsilon_r0),
    U=c(1+R)b.

Assume free electrons share the dust bulk velocity and the photons defining the CMB rest frame share the compensating radiation velocity. Then, at the electron event,

    beta_e-beta_r=(1+z+R)b
                 =[1+z/(1+R)]U/c.                     (1)

This is a physical relative velocity; a local boost of our detector cannot change it. It is not a model for unrestricted global tilt.

Let m be the sourceward direction on the electron's incident CMB sky. In the radiation rest frame the incident distribution is isotropic blackbody at this order. Expanding its exact local Lorentz pullback into the electron tetrad yields

    Theta_e(m,z)=m.(beta_e-beta_r)+O(beta²).

In particular, the normal-frame intrinsic photon dipole is -beta_r=R b, and the electron-frame boost adds beta_e. No SW or ISW term is generated by this strictly homogeneous mode with zero first-order metric perturbation. One must not replace the photon velocity by the velocity of the total-energy normal merely because that normal is FLRW.

Let n point from us toward the electron. Define the remote projected dipole directly by its angular moment:

    v_eff(n,z)=(3/4pi) int dOmega_m Theta_e(m,z)(n.m).

Using int m_i m_j dOmega=(4pi/3)delta_ij gives

    v_eff(n,z)=[1+z/(1+R)] n.U/c.                       (2)

Thus the physical velocity-valued remote field is w_eff=c v_eff. For optically thin single Thomson scattering,

    Theta_kSZ(n)=-int dchi dot_tau(n,chi)v_eff(n,z)
                =-(n.U/c) int dchi dot_tau(n,chi)
                                  [1+z/(1+R)].         (3)

The minus sign agrees with positive radial electron-relative-radiation velocity producing a temperature decrement. dot_tau dchi=d_tau is dimensionless; all terms in Theta are dimensionless. This result is first order in the coherent velocity mode and first order in optical depth.

The stochastic small-scale electron/galaxy distribution required for velocity reconstruction is an additional **test response** input. It is not supplied by the original strictly homogeneous two-fluid solution. Applying (3) with a fixed measured/modelled electron-density field is a constructive linear response in the coherent mode. Treating that field's cross-spectrum and its uncertainty as unchanged by the mode is an extra source assumption. Scattering backreaction, radiation drag, multiple scattering and accumulated perturbations of the homogeneous distribution have not been solved here.

Our local observer boost acts on the already propagated temperature and galaxy maps at their endpoint. It does not add beta_O to the electron-frame difference in (1). Its aberration, modulation and selection effects can nevertheless bias a practical quadratic reconstruction, so removal/calibration of those effects belongs to the measurement response; they are not proof that an endpoint boost produces a physical remote dipole.

If int W_v dchi=1, this simplifies to

    K_R=1+<z>_W/(1+R).                                 (5)

Hence the homogeneous compensated mode remains a pure angular dipole after any scalar radial window: the global direction is unchanged, and only its amplitude is rescaled. No new Boltzmann solver is required to establish this restricted map.

## 4. Harmonic dictionary and two conditional arithmetic conversions

For a unit-normalized spherical harmonic Y10=sqrt(3/(4pi))cos(theta) and the polar axis along U,

    w(n)=K_R |U| cos(theta)
        =D_h Y10(n),
    D_h=sqrt(4pi/3) K_R |U|.                            (6)

The rotation-invariant power-root amplitude is instead

    D_C=sqrt(C1)=sqrt[(1/3)sum_m|a_1m|²]
       =D_h/sqrt(3)=(sqrt(4pi)/3)K_R |U|.               (7)

Neither is the physical peak amplitude K_R|U|. These exact factors follow from the harmonic normalization; rotating the vector preserves the sum of squared harmonic coefficients.
