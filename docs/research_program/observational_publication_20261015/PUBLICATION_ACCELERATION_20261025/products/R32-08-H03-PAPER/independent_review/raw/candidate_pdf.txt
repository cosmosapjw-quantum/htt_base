Finite survey windows, correlated Hubble dipoles, and attainable response
discrepancies
Anonymous research draft
(Dated: 25 October 2026)
A uniform bulk velocity has zero divergence, yet an angularly incomplete distance survey can
mix that velocity into an apparent Hubble-rate fit. We use retained signed coefficients from four
overlapping redshift windows to compare a common bulk response with a depth-independent rate
dipole. Their combined fit leaves a quadratic discrepancy T = 23.3967205227 for six residual degrees of freedom, with conditional Gaussian tail 0.00067393342. Omitting cross-window covariance
changes the same fit to T = 8.925712183 and tail 0.177799. We then determine which corrections
can reach the unchanged 95% reference. A depth-slope design leaves an irreducible discrepancy
15.3160, above 12.5916; an FP-composition proxy and a joint response reach the reference at dimensionless coefficient radii 5.7509 and 1.5897. Finally, a calibration-quotiented CF4 tensor has
already dimensionless norm 0.0326568, which sets a concrete target for a future physical response
bound. These results specify finite-window response requirements under retained working statistics;
the coefficient budgets are design sensitivities rather than measured calibration errors.

I.

Directional distance–redshift coefficients connect kinematics to observations only through a specified observer and
source congruence [1, 2]. Survey depth and smoothing also enter the observed coefficients [3]. This distinction matters
when comparing an apparent Hubble dipole with a bulk-flow explanation [4]. We carry the actual finite-window
response into that comparison.

III.

OBSERVED RESPONSE FITS AND CROSS-WINDOW DEPENDENCE

We compare Dw = Rw b, Dw = d0 , and Dw = Rw b + d0 using the actual signed dipoles and bulk-response
matrices. The first two hypotheses each have three fitted coefficients; the last has six. Table I gives their
residual discrepancies. The bulk-only fit gives b = (−119.11, 57.68, −263.59) km s−1 . The combined fit gives
b = (−272.71, 118.35, −25.94) km s−1 and d0 = (1.4400, −0.6977, −2.4440) km s−1 Mpc−1 . The change in fitted
components illustrates their dependence on the admitted response space.
The full dipole covariance is a marginal of the full 36-coordinate carrier. Fitting those 36 coordinates and profiling
the remaining 24 mean coordinates freely gives the same statistic; conditioning on fixed measured monopole and STF
means would use a different covariance. The accompanying independent numerical check verifies the profiling identity.
The three working-law tails in Table I do not calibrate cosmological rarity or catalogue-wide null coverage.
Removing only cross-window blocks leaves the observed means unchanged but changes the combined tail from
0.00067393342 to 0.177799. Shared errors can cancel in depth differences, making those differences more precise than
the individual windows. Dropping dependence removes that cancellation. Figure 1 shows the signed coordinates and
finite-response fits; connecting the windows does not interpolate a motion history.

VI.

FINITE-WINDOW RESULTS

The depth response has three projected directions and leaves T⊥ = 15.3160 > τ even at infinite coefficient radius.
The three-column FP proxy leaves T⊥ = 6.6606 and crosses at ρ95 = 5.7509, with q95 = 1.7108. The six-column joint
design spans all six baseline residual directions numerically, leaves a floor below 2×10−28 , and crosses at ρ95 = 1.5897,
with q95 = 2.7480. The large ρsat values in Table II show that crossing and complete removal are distinct events. The
joint image-space minimum qmin = 1.2885530 equals the unrestricted radial comparator because the image spans the
six-dimensional residual space; its minimum-coefficient witness follows a different route and has the larger q95 .
The computation exports the full D and B matrices, singular spectra, whitened residual and nonspanning remainder,
threshold coefficient vectors, signed corrections, refitted b, d, and direct GLS residuals. The direct refits reproduce
the fixed threshold within 10−8 . Synthetic checks recover the wrong block-diagonal baseline, zero gain when D = X,
nonspanning floors at arbitrarily large budgets, finite equality when the floor equals the threshold, the unrestricted
radial formula when D = L, and invariance under a simultaneous Cartesian rotation. The computation uses the
original source hashes and no source reintegration.

VIII.

INTERPRETATION AND REQUIRED PHYSICAL INPUT

These results identify sensitivity of a retained finite-window fit to three explicit response spaces. The FP fractions
encode sample composition, not a signed calibration Jacobian; family-deletion dipole norms cannot supply such a
derivative. For an actual weighted least-squares estimator θ = N −1 b, a physical perturbation γ requires ∂γ θ =
N −1 (∂γ b − (∂γ N )θ), including any changed distances, design and weights. A source-bound signed window response to
named calibration, peculiar-velocity or selection parameters, together with a justified joint bound or law and shared
covariance, is needed to interpret η as a physical error. The sky, observer and cosmographic transfer, finite remainder
and null coverage are likewise open for stronger claims.
The result supplies a conditional structured-response frontier. Volume filling fractions, spatial exceedance probabilities, physical vorticity and global-tilt inference require additional physical response fields and a justified sampling
law; they cannot be recovered from this 12D statistic alone.

IX.

CONCLUSIONS

The actual finite-window responses distinguish a common translation from a constant apparent rate dipole under
the retained covariance. Their combination leaves T = 23.3967, whereas ignoring cross-window dependence gives
8.9257. The discrepancy has an explicit geometric interpretation: a depth slope misses part of the residual subspace,
while the composition and joint designs can reach the fixed reference at finite coefficient radii. Those radii quantify
a requirement on a proposed correction. They do not measure its physical amplitude.
A physical extension can now target named signed calibration or selection responses and their joint law, or bound
the response from a material velocity gradient to the apparent CF4 tensor. The retained norm gives a quantitative
scale for that task. These are finite observational-response questions which can be addressed without integrating a
native cosmological solver.
