        Structured finite-window response sensitivity in correlated Hubble dipoles

                                                  Anonymous research draft
                                                      (Dated: 25 October 2026)
                Four retained redshift-window dipoles carry a 12-component correlated discrepancy after fitting a
             bulk response and constant directional rate. We ask how much of this discrepancy can be moved by
             three prespecified finite-window response classes. Under the retained Gaussian working covariance,
             the baseline quadratic discrepancy is 23.3967, above the fixed six-degree 95% reference 12.5916. A
             depth-slope response leaves an irreducible 15.3160 and cannot reach that reference at any coefficient
             budget. An FP-composition proxy reaches it at a joint dimensionless coefficient radius 5.7509; a
             six-column combination reaches it at 1.5897. These are exact finite-dimensional sensitivity budgets
             for declared designs, not estimates of calibration error. We derive the image ellipsoid, its support
             and attainment, and the minimum-budget crossing including an irreducible-floor equality case.



                            I.   RETAINED CARRIER AND PHYSICAL QUESTION


   The saved four-window analysis represents each angular-rate estimator by a monopole, three Cartesian dipole coordi-
nates, and five signed symmetric trace-free coordinates in ICRS J2000. The centers are zw = (0.025, 0.040, 0.060, 0.080)
with saved width h = 0.012. Its full 36-by-36 joint covariance connects axes and windows, including shared groups.
We extract the marginal 12-by-12 dipole covariance C and window-major signed dipoles y; no conditioning on mea-
sured monopole or STF coordinates is performed. The complete physical coefficient carrier and source covariance are
retained in the accompanying source_carrier.json, alongside the extracted y, C and response matrices [1, 2]. This
extraction does not replace those tensors by a scalar.
   The retained estimator is a finite weighted angular-rate fit, czi = dDM,i [Hw + Dw · ni + nT

are in km s−1 Mpc−1 ; they are not themselves physical expansion coefficients. The source contains 35,970 groups,

                                         IV.   FINITE-WINDOW RESULTS

   The depth response has three projected directions and leaves T⊥ = 15.3160 > τ even at infinite coefficient radius.
The three-column FP proxy leaves T⊥ = 6.6606 and crosses at ρ95 = 5.7509, with q95 = 1.7108. The six-column joint
design spans all six baseline residual directions numerically, leaves a floor below 2×10−28 , and crosses at ρ95 = 1.5897,
with q95 = 2.7480. The large ρsat values in Table I show that crossing and complete removal are distinct events. The
joint image-space minimum qmin = 1.2885530 equals the unrestricted radial comparator because the image spans the
six-dimensional residual space; its minimum-coefficient witness follows a different route and has the larger q95 .
   The computation exports the full D and B matrices, singular spectra, whitened residual and nonspanning remainder,
threshold coefficient vectors, signed corrections, refitted b, d, and direct GLS residuals. The direct refits reproduce
the fixed threshold within 10−8 . Synthetic checks recover the wrong block-diagonal baseline, zero gain when D = X,
nonspanning floors at arbitrarily large budgets, finite equality when the floor equals the threshold, the unrestricted
radial formula when D = L, and invariance under a simultaneous Cartesian rotation. The computation uses the
original source hashes and no source reintegration.

                                   V.   INTERPRETATION AND REQUIRED PHYSICAL INPUT


  These results identify sensitivity of a retained finite-window fit to three explicit response spaces. The FP fractions
encode sample composition, not a signed calibration Jacobian; family-deletion dipole norms cannot supply such a
derivative. For an actual weighted least-squares estimator θ = N −1 b, a physical perturbation γ requires ∂γ θ =
N −1 (∂γ b − (∂γ N )θ), including any changed distances, design and weights. A source-bound signed window response to
named calibration, peculiar-velocity or selection parameters, together with a justified joint bound or law and shared
covariance, is needed to interpret η as a physical error. The sky, observer and cosmographic transfer, finite remainder
and null coverage are likewise open for stronger claims.
