    Finite-bandpass CMB identifiability with bounded foreground moments

         Frequency separation of the cosmic microwave background depends on the foreground class
       admitted by the analysis. We examine seven released Planck passband responses acting on
       the complete signed E2 , E3 , B2 , B3 harmonic carrier. The declared first-order synchrotron–
       dust class leaves all 24 target coordinates spectrally identifiable in the finite model. Adding
       four free second-order moment maps gives a 7 × 9 nuisance response of resolved rank seven
       and removes the entire target quotient. A fixed joint quadratic coefficient bound nevertheless
       gives a sharp compensation frontier. We state the minimum compensator, the complete
       compatible target set and its coefficient fibres, retaining the two nuisance null directions. The
       stored minimum budget for a unit target displacement is 3.117720–3.118388 in a common
       column-normalized metric, and 59.572829–59.592192 in a separately declared raw-coordinate
       metric. A single compensator has radius ρ/ℓM ; two foregrounds, each bounded by ρ, can
       hide a separation as large as 2ρ/ℓM . These conclusions concern fixed finite response matrices
       and free signed moments. They supply no observed CMB amplitude, physical foreground
       bound or noise-calibrated region.



             I.   THE FOREGROUND CLASS DETERMINES THE QUESTION


   A calibrated frequency map responds to the CMB and to foreground emission after spectral av-
eraging. Averaging spectra with different parameters introduces additional spectral shapes, as made
explicit by the moment expansion of Chluba, Hill and Abitbol [1]. Constrained linear combinations
preserve a target response while cancelling declared nuisance responses [2]; constrained moment
methods apply this construction to foreground moments [3]. The possibility of such cancellation is
a question about the chosen response space before it is a question about statistical precision.
   We study that space for seven released Planck passbands and two nested, stipulated foreground
classes. The first contains five synchrotron and dust columns. The second adds four second-order
columns and allows their harmonic coefficients to vary independently. This enlarged linear class is
a relaxation of a physical moment model. A positive intensity, a nonlinear component spectrum
or a truncation remainder can impose relations absent from the relaxation. We do not infer those
relations from a matrix rank.
   The finite calculation has two distinct conclusions. With unrestricted second-order coefficients,
the nuisance response spans every frequency row. With a specified bound on those coefficients, a
target displacement has a nonzero minimum cost. We retain both statements, including the metric
that defines the bound. No map fit is needed to formulate this question.

              VI.    SHARP FRONTIERS AND COORDINATE INVARIANCE


   For zero data, s0 = 0 and J0 = 0. One bounded foreground hides a target displacement precisely
when ∥v∥ ≤ ρ/ℓM . For two foregrounds each bounded by ρ, choose

                     s1 = v/2,     s2 = −v/2,      U1 = −cM v T /2,     U2 = cM v T /2.         (16)

Both give Y = 0, and each has budget ℓM ∥v∥/2. Conversely, F (U2 − U1 ) = a(s1 − s2 )T and
minimality imply

                                    ℓM ∥s1 − s2 ∥ ≤ ∥U2 − U1 ∥M ≤ 2ρ.                           (17)

                 VII.    AN ADDITIONAL FINITE-RESPONSE COMPARISON


   A separately checked WMAP–Planck extension reuses the seven nominal Planck rows and adds
stipulated WMAP response averages. It distinguishes 12 instrument-band-average rows from 17
differencing-assembly response rows; the latter are not 17 independent frequencies. Across its 12
paired conventions, the nine-column nuisance matrix has resolved rank nine and the augmented
matrix rank ten in both row constructions, retaining all 24 target coordinates in the finite model.
The stored independent check gives a minimum principal-angle sine of approximately 3.6284 × 10−5
and maximum Euclidean weight norm 7955.99. This is a severe conditioning warning. There is no
supplied actual noise law or polarized spatial-response model from which to establish useful sky
separation. The extension changes the finite response geometry; it does not replace the seven-row
result or the coefficient class studied above.

                                              VIII.    PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION AND REMAINING INPUTS


                    At zero budget, U = 0 and compatible data must equal asT . Exact pure-target data therefore
give a singleton at ρ = 0, and a full ball at positive budget. As ρ grows without bound, the
unrestricted second-order loss of the target quotient is recovered. These limits give a direct reading
of the frontier: it is the cost of compensation in a stipulated coefficient class.
                    The independent signed coordinates do not enforce physical realizability. Positivity of an un-
derlying intensity does not require positive polarization harmonics or positive derivative moments.
A physical restriction needs supplied moment magnitudes and couplings, a truncation-remainder
bound, component-specific spatial responses, and a justified metric and radius. A noise-calibrated
region additionally needs the actual joint noise and calibration law. A nuisance with the exact
CMB blackbody response remains spectrally confounded.
                    No observed target tensor or physical anisotropy parameter is measured here. In particular,
full-tensor nonlinear quantities remain undefined in sectors where the target is unobserved or the
required denominators are absent. A frequency axis supplies no depth information. The finite
response results do not infer a local or global tilt, identify a Bianchi family, or validate a native
cosmological solver. Their use is to specify which additional response directions or physically
