Finite CMB compensation budgets in a bounded nine-moment foreground class

                                Anonymous methods and results note

         Seven retained passband responses give a full-row-rank matrix for nine signed, second-order
       synchrotron and dust moments in each of six fixed response conventions. In the unrestricted
       linear class, the CMB response can consequently be represented by foreground moments in
       every one of the 24 signed E2 /E3 /B2 /B3 coordinates. A declared joint quadratic bound
       changes that statement quantitatively: the least moment budget for a unit CMB response
       is 3.117720–3.118388 target-amplitude units in one common column-normalized metric. A
       single foreground can compensate a target tensor displacement of norm at most ρ/ℓM , while
       two bounded foregrounds can make two targets separated by as much as 2ρ/ℓM agree on the
       same synthetic data. For arbitrary specified frequency data, the complete 24-dimensional
       compatible target set is a calculable ball or is empty. The raw-coordinate identity metric
       changes the unit cost to 59.572829–59.592192, displaying explicit metric sensitivity. These
       are finite algebraic results for the declared free signed-moment class. No sky tensor, physical
       moment bound, joint noise law, or native solver output is used.



            I.   PHYSICAL MECHANISM AND FROZEN FINITE RESPONSE


   A passband integrates spectral components before their angular coefficients are estimated. Spec-
tral changes in a component’s index or temperature therefore add frequency response columns to
its leading spectrum. If enough independently variable moment maps are admitted, their channel
responses can reproduce the CMB’s calibrated response. A bound on their joint amplitude restores
a finite compensation cost, even when the unrestricted response matrix spans all channels. That
cost depends on which moment amplitudes the bound regards as comparable.
   This note consumes the already computed, released-bandpass finite matrices in the retained R31
result identified in the accompanying claim-to-source map; it performs no passband integration or
map analysis. The seven frequency rows, in order, are 030, 044, 070, 100, 143, 217, and 353PSB.

We evaluate J0 from a whitened residual equivalent to Eq. (8), avoiding subtraction of nearly equal
quadratic forms. Since the retained CMB response is exactly seven ones, exactly repeated finite
rows of Y have s0 equal to their common row and J0 = 0; at ρ = 0 any exactly unequal rows give
an empty set. Thus exact pure-target data give a singleton at zero budget and a full 24-dimensional
ball of radius ρ/ℓM at positive budget. For other data, a homogeneous squared-amplitude error
policy covers the factorization, triangular solves and projection. A resolved J0 alone does not certify
a returned ball: its serialized center, residual, radius, ℓM and 24-coordinate boundary image must
also clear the same relative budget policy. An unresolved boundary or output geometry has status
BOUNDARY_UNCERTAIN, NUMERIC_BOUNDARY_HOLD, or NUMERIC_RANGE_HOLD and no asserted radius;
positive radii that cannot be serialized also receive a range hold. This numerical policy is not
a general interval certificate. Equation (9) is a deterministic compatible set, not a posterior or
confidence region.
   The zero-data illustration Y = 0 has s0 = 0 and J0 = 0. One foreground can hide v exactly
when ℓM ∥v∥ ≤ ρ, with U = −cM v T . For two foregrounds, each separately bounded by ρ, a common
zero data table is obtained from

                     s1 = v/2,     s2 = −v/2,       U1 = −cM v T /2,     U2 = cM v T /2.          (10)

Each budget equals ℓM ∥v∥/2. Conversely F (U2 − U1 ) = a(s1 − s2 ) and the triangle inequality give
ℓM ∥s1 − s2 ∥ ≤ ∥U2 ∥M + ∥U1 ∥M ≤ 2ρ. Thus the factor of two is both necessary and attained.

   The moment class permits signed independent harmonic and derivative coordinates. Physical
component intensities, if positive, do not imply positivity of these harmonic or derivative coef-
ficients. A physical version would need externally justified magnitude, coupling and truncation-
remainder constraints, component-specific spatial responses, a real joint noise/calibration model,
and a justified radius or prior. Those inputs are absent here. A nuisance with exactly the blackbody
response remains confounded under this spectral model. We therefore make no statement about
an observed quotient tensor, actual CMB amplitude, physical foreground filling fraction, calibrated
exceedance, global tilt or Bianchi family. The retained status fields are x = NOT_MEASURED,
Q = NOT_EVALUATED, Fphysical = NOT_EVALUABLE, Π = NOT_CALIBRATED, and
GF = NOT_APPLICABLE because frequency is not depth. Complete source identities and claim
