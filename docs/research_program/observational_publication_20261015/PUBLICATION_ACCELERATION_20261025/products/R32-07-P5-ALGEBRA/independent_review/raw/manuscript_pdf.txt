  Admissible shear–tilt images in LRS Bianchi V and a radiation-history degeneracy

               Momentum and energy constraints couple the curvature, shear and tilt of a homogeneous space-
            time before evolution is specified. For an oriented, expanding, locally rotationally symmetric Bianchi
            V slice containing one perfect fluid and a nonnegative cosmological constant, we give a complete
            parameterization of its instantaneous algebraically admissible region. A rationalized quadratic root
            yields the allowed rest-density interval while preserving the signed shear, fluid flux and anisotropic
            stress. The same construction gives a sharp tilt bound at fixed matter density and curvature, finite-
            tilt shear envelopes, and an ultrarelativistic supremum. We then exhibit two smooth collisionless
            Bianchi I metric histories with identical full final angular temperature functions and different in-
            stantaneous shear. Their endpoint shear amplitudes differ by a factor of 9/5. This exact kinematic
            example identifies the history information lost by that radiation observation map. The results spec-
            ify which deterministic images can be formed from the stated geometry and radiation assumptions,
            and which additional dynamical and observational inputs would connect those images.

                                             VI.    PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION AND SCOPE

   The LRS Bianchi V calculation shows how curvature and momentum couple a signed shear to a rest-density and tilt
state. Its allowed set is a joint instantaneous constraint image. The Bianchi I example shows that even a complete final
temperature pattern can leave instantaneous shear nonunique under a specified kinematic radiation map. Connecting
a measured sky to the Bianchi V bounds therefore requires a source-matched history response, together with its
matter, propagation and observer assumptions.
   All plotted images and error bounds in this note are deterministic. There is no empirical sky, mask, covariance,
null ensemble, parameter probability law, likelihood or posterior. The only radiation transfer used is the explicit
collisionless map (27); no native solver, external transfer calibration, mode atlas or polarization map is used. The
calculation establishes neither a Bianchi family identification nor an all-m temperature/polarization prediction for
the Bianchi V sector. An external atlas is unnecessary for the conditional algebraic claims proved here, while stronger
history and identification claims retain their own missing inputs.
   The tensors in (12) are defined carriers, and Σ2 is a geometric scalar within the stated normal frame. A signed
xC requires a specified same-frame comparator with explicit energy and curvature subtraction. A normalized Q
score requires its anchor and denominator; F requires a support measure and appropriate ceiling; Π requires a joint
probability law; and GF requires supported depth-dependent F reports and their dependence. Those inputs are
absent, so these quantities are not evaluated. The radiation quadrupole tensor, when mentioned as QT , is distinct
from the normalized Q score. Normal vorticity is exactly zero by hypersurface orthogonality; matter-frame vorticity
remains undetermined.
