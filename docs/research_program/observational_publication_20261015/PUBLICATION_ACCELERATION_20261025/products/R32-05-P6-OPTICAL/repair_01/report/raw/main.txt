   Finite optical inverses and the error radius required for a shear constraint

                                  Conditional methods review draft

         A local distance law encodes expansion, acceleration and shear, but a catalogue measures
       that law through selected directions and nuisance projections. We compose these operations
       and derive the exact identifiable sector of the resulting finite linear response. Its inverse
       is a set: measurement and response errors determine a residual-compatible image, while
       the response kernel remains free. We give its support function, a stable inverse bound,
       and an explicit tensor ellipsoid with an additional bounded response term. For the com-
       plete positive angular magnitude field, normalized exponentiation instead gives an exact
       intercept-independent inverse; a uniform field error yields a finite bound on the recovered
       dipole and shear. These two inverses have different domains. A retained CF4 calibration
       quotient illustrates the finite construction with five dimensionless tensor coordinates, norm
       0.0326568018932, and principal values approximately -0.0237272, 0.00132795 and 0.0223992.
       Conditional shear bounds become positive only below the apparent tensor norm. We show
       their dependence on an assumed response-error radius and Hubble normalization, retaining
       the tensor region and the physical sectors absent from the local slope.




                  I.   WHAT THE LOCAL DISTANCE SLOPE CONTAINS


   The first distance coefficient is a directional rate. Its physical content depends on the source
congruence and observer, while its recovery depends on how the directions and distances were
sampled. A finite tensor estimate therefore poses two questions: which combinations survive the
observation operator, and which physical states remain compatible with its errors? We answer these
questions with a composed response and a set-valued inverse.

   General luminosity-distance cosmography expresses the local coefficients through finitely many
kinematic multipoles [1]. Observer transformations must also be carried through the distance
and redshift observables [2]. We use the same-congruence local law and logarithmic remainder
established in the companion optical construction [3]. Our finite result retains the sampling and
nuisance operators before attempting an inverse. Its error regions are deterministic sets unless a
joint sampling law is explicitly supplied.

                                          VII.     DISCUSSION


   There are two useful inverses. A complete positive local magnitude field determines its dimen-
sionless acceleration and shear after normalized exponentiation. A finite observation first passes
through sampling and nuisance removal, leaving an identifiable subspace, a kernel and a residual-
compatibility condition. Their error bounds answer different questions, so the full-field identity
cannot be substituted into an incomplete map without controlling the missing field.
   The retained CF4 quotient makes the error target concrete.                       Its apparent tensor norm is
0.0326568 in the producer’s dimensionless convention. To obtain a positive lower shear bound
through the stated response identity, a qualified common-event radius must lie below this scale. A
supplied response region can be propagated with (18) or (21), retaining anisotropy and dependence
before taking the simpler norm bounds. An absolute Hubble interval then converts a dimensionless
shear range to a dimensional rate.
   This construction isolates what an additional physical input must do: constrain the response
error or a currently unresolved sector. It also permits a complete conditional inverse result while
those stronger inputs remain unavailable. The attainable sets and their support functions are the
methods result; the apparent tensor is its retained finite-data illustration.
