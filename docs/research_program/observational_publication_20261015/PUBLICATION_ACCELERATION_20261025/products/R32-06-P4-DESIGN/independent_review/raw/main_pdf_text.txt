                                             Abstract
     A radial velocity response measures the difference between source bulk motion and observer
motion. A time-integrated channel also loses any history mode orthogonal to its window. We con-
struct a finite example in which these two ambiguities can be separated explicitly. Thirteen radial
responses at two radii determine twelve combinations of eighteen signed coefficients after removing
a scalar calibration. Their six-dimensional kernel consists of a common observer–bulk displacement
and an unweighted history mode. An independently calibrated three-component observer response
removes the first ambiguity, while a second normalized history window removes the second. Either
addition raises the rank to fifteen; together they give rank eighteen. Equal radii provide a negative
control: the joint rank falls to fourteen. A single, fixed column normalization permits compar-
isons of all four designs, radial leverage and channel precision. Singular-value bounds quantify the
deterministic error amplification and the response accuracy required for a stable inverse. The con-
struction is a conditional measurement design with prescribed response functions and calibration,
suitable for evaluating which additional information a physical history provider must supply.

III.   EXACT RANKS AND IDENTIFIED COMBINATIONS


   Proposition 1 (two-radius separation). For (7), the nine-dimensional spherical poly-
nomial space consisting of a monopole, a dipole and an STF quadrupole is unisolvent. With
the four repeated directions in (7) placed at any distinct positive radius q ̸= 1, the matrix


                       X = [E4 , RE9 ],       E4 = [1, E3 ],    E9 = [1, E3 , Q5 ]               (14)

   has rank thirteen.
   Proof. Let f (n) = a + d · n + nT T n, T STF, vanish on the first nine directions. The
opposite axis pairs first give d = 0, then a + Tii = 0. Taking the trace gives a = 0 and
all three diagonal entries vanish. At the three remaining directions the nonzero cross terms
force Txy = Txz = Tyz = 0. Hence E9 is invertible. A degree-one polynomial vanishing at
ex , −ex , ey , ez is likewise zero, so the repeated-direction E4 is invertible. A null vector of (14)
represents polynomials p1 + ρp2 . At ρ = 1, unisolvence gives p2 = −p1 as polynomials. At
the four repeated directions the equation becomes (1 − q)p1 = 0, forcing p1 = p2 = 0. This
is the retained two-radius rank argument, expressed in the orthonormal tensor coordinates
(2). □

VII.   PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION AND THE NEXT MEASUREMENT


   The design identifies what must change when a baseline response cannot separate physical
mechanisms. The observer–bulk ambiguity requires a response to observer motion that
is not a response to the same source bulk displacement. The history ambiguity requires
a window with nonzero overlap with a temporal mode invisible to the existing window.
These requirements are independent of the number of rows carrying the unchanged baseline
response. Radial leverage performs a third job: separating angular intercepts from rate and
mean-history coefficients.
   For a physical implementation, the carrier remains the signed vectors (ℓ, b, g0 , g1 ), the full
STF tensor S, scalar m, response functions and their joint error set in one frame. The ra-
dial response contains no antisymmetric velocity-gradient measurement, because nT Ωn = 0.
Matter vorticity therefore remains NOT_MEASURED; a chosen hypersurface normal’s irrota-
tionality is a distinct statement. The scalar rate and fitted STF coefficients in this design
do not alone provide a validated physical expansion/shear bridge.
