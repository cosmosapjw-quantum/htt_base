A useful common observation equation is

\[
 y=\mathcal M\,\mathcal T_\eta[X(\cdot)]+N\nu+\epsilon.
 \tag{U1}
\]

Here \(X\) is the physical state, \(\mathcal T_\eta\) transports it to emitted or received radiation and source motion, and \(\mathcal M\) applies the actual selection, beam, mask, distance calibration and estimator. The nuisance term \(N\nu\) describes admitted alternatives in the same observation space. The tensor representation makes the directions and signs available for this comparison. It does not remove either operator. In particular, a sky STF tensor, a dimensionless fitted rate tensor and a dimensional congruence shear all transform under spatial rotations, while their physical units and responses differ.

This construction connects to general relativistic cosmography: the directional coefficients of a local distance–redshift expansion can carry expansion, acceleration and shear information [P1,P2]. The practical survey defines a smoothing and selection window, so its fitted coefficients can depend strongly on source depth [P3]. ### 13.1 Three coefficient conventions that must stay distinct

Let \(T\) denote a dimensionless directional-rate STF tensor and \(\kappa=5/\ln10\). At first order in the directional perturbation,

\[

 \tag{U2}
\]

The 274-row selected-law analysis in Sections 6–7 fits a magnitude tensor against raw STF angular columns. Its coefficient is \(B_\mu\). H03 fits \(cz=d[H+D\cdot n+B_H:nn]\), giving rates in km s\(^{-1}\) Mpc\(^{-1}\). The new 35,970-group quotient places \(-\kappa\) in the magnitude design matrix and directly estimates \(T\). We checked the last convention against the sealed producer, whose STF basis starts with \(\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2\) and \(\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-2)/\sqrt6\). Some accompanying R28 framing labels instead say magnitude and reverse the second basis sign. The numerical decoded tensor agrees with the producer. We therefore preserve the arrays and correct the physical labels. Applying \(-\ln10/5\) once more would be a second, erroneous conversion. This distinction also preserves the earlier manuscript's genuinely magnitude-valued coefficients.

### 13.2 A constructive physical inference target

The immediate target is a permitted set of motion and geometry, with a forward prediction for each measured coefficient. A model whose image misses the observational region can be excluded within its response assumptions; a model whose image overlaps remains compatible. A unique physical interpretation requires the fibre of compatible states to be sufficiently narrow for the requested functional. This gives a useful intermediate result even when the entire spacetime history cannot be reconstructed.

For example, one can ask whether every compatible state has a positive principal shear, whether a bulk-only response suffices across distance windows, or how large a residual transport error must be before a shear lower bound vanishes. These questions use actual measured values and physically typed inequalities. They are stronger than describing a map as unusual, while asking less than complete recovery of an Einstein–matter solution.

## 20. A unified physical output and the next discriminating measurements

The useful final object is the image of one permitted physical set under several observation maps. \[

Every requested quantity \(h(X)\) then has a directly defined identified range \(h(\mathcal X_{\mathfrak F})\).
