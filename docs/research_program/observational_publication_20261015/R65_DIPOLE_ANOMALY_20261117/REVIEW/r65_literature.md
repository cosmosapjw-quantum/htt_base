## Primary comparison and source-to-target transport

1. **Ellis & Baldwin (1984), On the expected anisotropy of radio source counts**, MNRAS206,377–381; original primary scan read: https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1984MNRAS.206..377E . Printed p379 gives counts multiplied by delta^[2+x(1+alpha)], with delta=1+beta·n+O(beta²). Therefore d_kin=[2+x(1+alpha)]beta. Applicable to isotropic unresolved source population and identical forward/backward selection in the stated flux/spectral approximation. Original paper already emphasizes sub-percent angular calibration. This is a response prediction, not evidence that every residual is observer motion.

2. **Wagenveld et al. (2025), The kinematic contribution to the cosmic number count dipole**, A&A697,A112, DOI10.1051/0004-6361/202453397; full primary text read: https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2025/05/aa53397-24/aa53397-24.html . Eqs4–5: lambda_ij=M_j(1+d_j·n_i) and pixel Poisson likelihood; Eq8: d_j=[2+x_j(1+alpha_j)]beta+d_resid. NVSS/RACS/CatWISE selections, masks, measured near-threshold slopes and spectral indices are specified in Sec3. The common residual assumption is a scientific restriction, not a consequence of cosmological tilt. Fixed x,alpha uncertainties were not propagated (authors explicitly acknowledge this). Free boost plus free residual direction/amplitude failed to separate with current samples; fixing CMB boost gives a conditional residual. Removing shared sources addresses identical-object shot noise but does not prove all cosmological cross-covariance vanishes. Code: https://github.com/JonahDW/Bayesian-dipole ; DOI10.5281/zenodo.7962922; current README includes March2026 negative-binomial option, v0.3 March2025 combined modes. It accepts HEALPix FITS directly or catalogues via JSON parsets, enabling reuse of existing root count maps. A March2025 archived version is preferable for exact paper reproduction; current main is a different software identity.

3. **Takeuchi (2026), A General Formulation of the Kinematic Dipole as a Functional of Selection and Source Properties: Beyond the Ellis–Baldwin Approximation**, arXiv2602.07389v2 (2026-05-08); explicit v2 primary HTML read: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07389v2 . Eqs22–27 give

   n(n)=delta² integral f(y) W(y_delta,n)dy,
   n0(n)=integral f(y)W(y,n)dy,
   R(n)=integral f(y)[dW(y_delta,n)/d ln delta]_(delta=1)dy,
   delta n/n0=[2+R(n)/n0(n)] beta·n.

   Eqs30–31 give bandpass/SED q_b=d ln S_b/d ln delta; Eq32 finite-catalogue response; Eq34 chain rule includes color/photo-z/classifier boundaries. Parent population is statistically isotropic with no intrinsic dipole. A direction-dependent response multiplied by beta·n need not remain a pure l=1 field; apply the actual mask/estimator before calling its fitted vector a scalar response times beta. A truncated science catalogue alone generally cannot certify threshold-crossing response: need deeper parent objects or a justified forward population/noise model. This is direct prior art for generic response calibration; novelty must be actual sample-specific implementation and measurement.

4. **von Hausegger & Dalang, Redshift tomography of the kinematic matter dipole**, arXiv2412.13162v2; primary read: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13162v2 . Eq20 contains the usual selected-sample EB coefficient plus endpoint and smooth-selection terms; Eq21 B_b=-integral dz f_b(z) d ln W_b/d ln(1+z), with f_b the normalized selected redshift distribution. Sharp-cut limit Eq23 is [(1+z)f_b(z)]_(z1)^(z2), when outer endpoints vanish. Source redshift and color selection both move under boost. This is prior art for boundary response; color cuts cannot be assumed boost-invariant merely because the final catalogue has no spectroscopic z.

5. **Bonnefous (2026), The kinematic cosmic dipole beyond Ellis and Baldwin**, A&A full text read: https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2026/04/aa59275-26/aa59275-26.html . Eq24 alpha_eff=-[integral dnu T_X nu partial_nu S_lim]/[integral dnu T_X S_lim]=1+[integral dnu (partial_nu T_X)nu S_lim]/[integral dnu T_X S_lim], assuming boundary term vanishes. The limiting-source spectrum and band response matter. Its proof-of-concept uses only41 bright AKARI quasars (>5mJy, positive fitted alpha), not a representative faint CatWISE flux-limit spectroscopic population. Three alpha methods agree within considerable uncertainty; possible ~0.16 offset changes EB response only ~4% in their example. It does not establish every CatWISE selection/noise correction is small. General bandpass formula is prior art.

6. **Mittal & Lewis (2026), The Ellis and Baldwin test of the Cosmic Dipole: Exploring the impact of multiple flux density cuts**, arXiv2605.27520, JCAP2026/07/081; https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27520 . Exact formula/code implementation not claimed read here. Root separately found the same source. Do not claim novelty for fitting a common observer speed across flux bins alone.

## Finite R3 closest-prior addendum: declination-resistant transverse dipoles

Scope completed: exactly three targeted Exa searches, followed by full primary methods inspection of the closest two retrieved papers; no expanded review or new fit. This addendum refines the novelty gate after root fixed its actual experiment: NVSS disjoint **peak-intensity** bins20–40/40–80/80+ mJy/beam, conditional HEALPix-ring multinomial log intensity ux nx+uy ny, with optional four non-axisymmetric STF quadrupole components and full signed covariance/path. Peak intensity is not automatically the integrated point-source flux assumed in EB response; morphology/resolution and source extraction enter its velocity/selection response.

**Closest exact prior: Rubart & Schwarz2013, Cosmic radio dipole from NVSS and WENSS**, A&A555,A117, DOI10.1051/0004-6361/201321215; full primary text read https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2013/07/aa21215-13/aa21215-13.html ; arXiv1301.5559v3. Their Eq31 is

    R_2D=sum_i (cos(phi_i)sin(theta_i), sin(phi_i)sin(theta_i), 0)
         =sum_i (cos(RA_i)cos(Dec_i), sin(RA_i)cos(Dec_i), 0).

Eqs28–30 derive the incomplete-hemisphere monopole/dipole normalization and transverse orientation; Eqs32–38 treat 2D random-walk amplitude bias and uncertainties. Sec7.2 explicitly proposes the 2D estimator to avoid NVSS's change in sampling depth below Dec=-10deg; Sec7.2.1 and Table5 measure right ascension and transverse amplitude over several flux thresholds. To restore symmetry around the north pole they remove the Galactic and counter-Galactic planes. Their amplitude masking correction still uses an estimated dipole declination from the3D estimator. Consequently **equatorial projection, sacrificing the axial component, testing flux dependence and resistance to declination-only density systematics are already direct prior art**. This paper's estimator is a projected linear vector sum with geometry/amplitude calibration; it is not the actual ring-conditional loglinear likelihood now selected by root.

**Independent later qualification: Siewert, Schmidt-Rubart & Schwarz2021, Cosmic radio dipole: Estimators and frequency dependence**, full primary text read https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2021/09/aa39840-20/aa39840-20.html . AppendixA EqA4 reproduces the same linear2DCG estimator and EqA5 its mask/amplitude correction. Sec3, Eqs10–24, derives directional bias caused by a dipole interacting with masks: cancelling masked monopole alone does not guarantee unbiased direction. Their quadratic count-map method is intended to handle actual mask geometry. This prevents transporting the2013 orientation discussion as an all-mask invariance theorem. It supports root's use of the actual available ring pixels and normalization rather than projecting a generic full-sky error law.

**Source-to-target map, derived here and not novelty claimed.** Under root's model of independent pixel Poisson counts,

    lambda_ri=a_r E_ri exp(u_x n_x,ri+u_y n_y,ri+q·T_ri),

conditioning on each retained ring total N_r removes arbitrary positive a_r exactly:

    P({n_ri}|N_r,u,q)=Multinomial(N_r,{p_ri}),
    p_ri=E_ri exp(u·n_perp,ri+q·T_ri)/sum_j E_rj exp(u·n_perp,rj+q·T_rj).

The score is sum_ri[n_ri-N_r p_ri]n_perp,ri, i.e. a mask/exposure/ring-normalized transverse contrast; at zero parameters it centers the projected vector sum within every ring. This is mathematically related to the old transverse estimator but uses conditional normalization, not its full-sky random-flight law. Any exactly ring-constant log-intensity term cancels, including u_z n_z and the axisymmetric STF quadrupole at fixedHEALPix ring z; the retained four quadrupole components vary with RA. This is exact for the stated loglinear independent-Poisson model and discrete rings, not for arbitrary angular calibration or correlated LSS. A density law1+d·n and exp(u·n) agree only to first order; do not identify their finite amplitudes without a transformation/approximation statement.
