              astrometric response design

     A radial velocity response measures the difference between source bulk motion and observer mo-
tion. A time-integrated channel also loses any history mode orthogonal to its window. We first
construct a finite example in which independently calibrated observer and history responses remove
these two ambiguities. Thirteen radial measurements at two radii determine twelve combinations of
eighteen signed coefficients after removing a scalar calibration. Either additional three-component
response raises the rank to fifteen; together they give rank eighteen. Equal radii reduce the joint
rank to fourteen. We then examine a proper-motion response whose five-component trace-free ten-
sor combines a source–observer endpoint difference with the present observer-time derivative. The
associated ten-coordinate history map has rank five even when the observable tensor is fully deter-
mined. For a retained sample of 755,850 astrometric sources, we compare six common spin/glide
nuisance coefficients with all 97 coefficients outside the common electric-quadrupole signal span,
using the same complete normal matrix and score. Both projected tensor responses have rank five.
Under the stipulated Gaussian noise law, their worst-direction tensor radii for 80 percent power
are 2.2943 and 5.0499 microarcseconds per year, respectively. These are conditional design sen-
sitivities. Their interpretation as matter shear requires a physical distance/history response, an
optical-centroid transfer and a calibrated spatial error model.

observer response removes the observer–bulk ambiguity, while a second temporal window
removes a history moment invisible to the first. Radial leverage remains necessary: at equal
radii four parameter combinations survive even after both additions. These conclusions
follow from the stated response functions and common nuisance quotient.
