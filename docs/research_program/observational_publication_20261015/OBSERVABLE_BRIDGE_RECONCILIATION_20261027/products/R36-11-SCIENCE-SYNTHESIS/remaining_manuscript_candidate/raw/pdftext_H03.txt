               A uniform bulk velocity has zero divergence, yet an angularly incomplete distance survey can
            mix that velocity into an apparent Hubble-rate fit. We use retained signed coefficients from four
            overlapping redshift windows to compare a common bulk response with a depth-independent rate
            dipole. Their combined fit leaves a quadratic discrepancy T = 23.3967205227 for six residual de-
            grees of freedom, with conditional Gaussian tail 0.00067393342. Omitting cross-window covariance
            changes the same fit to T = 8.925712183 and tail 0.177799. We then determine which corrections
            can reach the unchanged 95% reference. A depth-slope design leaves an irreducible discrepancy
            15.3160, above 12.5916; an FP-composition proxy and a joint response reach the reference at di-
            mensionless coefficient radii 5.7509 and 1.5897. Finally, a calibration-quotiented CF4 tensor has
            already dimensionless norm 0.0326568, which sets a concrete target for a future physical response
            bound. These results specify finite-window response requirements under retained working statis-
            tics; the coefficient budgets are design sensitivities rather than measured calibration errors. We also
            propagate a common Gaussian velocity field through the exact finite-window estimator, retaining its
            cross-window and cross-smoothing covariance under an explicit restricted-band model. A separate
            two-depth Pantheon+ magnitude comparison retains all signed dipole and quadrupole coordinates
            and gives a joint improvement of 17.2066 for 16 angular coefficients, with conditional upper-tail
            probability 0.37235 under the released selected-covariance Gaussian law.


                          I.     MOTION, EXPANSION AND THE SURVEY WINDOW

source congruence [1, 2]. Survey depth and smoothing also enter the observed coefficients [3]. This distinction matters
when comparing an apparent Hubble dipole with a bulk-flow explanation [4]. We carry the actual finite-window
response into that comparison.

   We compare Dw = Rw b, Dw = d0 , and Dw = Rw b + d0 using the actual signed dipoles and bulk-response

   The full dipole covariance is a marginal of the full 36-coordinate carrier. Fitting those 36 coordinates and profiling

                                                           X.     INTERPRETATION AND REQUIRED PHYSICAL INPUT

   These results identify sensitivity of a retained finite-window fit to three explicit response spaces. The FP fractions
encode sample composition, not a signed calibration Jacobian; family-deletion dipole norms cannot supply such a
derivative. For an actual weighted least-squares estimator θ = N −1 b, a physical perturbation γ requires ∂γ θ =
N −1 (∂γ b − (∂γ N )θ), including any changed distances, design and weights. A source-bound signed window response to
named calibration, peculiar-velocity or selection parameters, together with a justified joint bound or law and shared
covariance, is needed to interpret η as a physical error. The sky, observer and cosmographic transfer, finite remainder
and null coverage are likewise open for stronger claims.
   The result supplies a conditional structured-response frontier. Volume filling fractions, spatial exceedance proba-
bilities, physical vorticity and global-tilt inference require additional physical response fields and a justified sampling
law; they cannot be recovered from this 12D statistic alone.


                                                                                       XI.      CONCLUSIONS

  The actual finite-window responses distinguish a common translation from a constant apparent rate dipole under
the retained covariance. Their combination leaves T = 23.3967, whereas ignoring cross-window dependence gives
8.9257. The discrepancy has an explicit geometric interpretation: a depth slope misses part of the residual subspace,
while the composition and joint designs can reach the fixed reference at finite coefficient radii. Those radii quantify
a requirement on a proposed correction. They do not measure its physical amplitude.

   A physical extension can now target named signed calibration or selection responses and their joint law, or bound
the response from a material velocity gradient to the apparent CF4 tensor. The retained norm gives a quantitative
scale for that task. These are finite observational-response questions which can be addressed without integrating a
native cosmological solver.
   The additional covariance transport demonstrates how a shared velocity field changes the joint apparent-coefficient
uncertainty through the actual survey operator. The released-magnitude comparison gives a distinct observational
application with its own angular response and covariance. Their physical combination requires source, observer-
frame and cross-survey error models; those requirements cannot be replaced by treating the two apparent tensors as
measurements of one material shear.
