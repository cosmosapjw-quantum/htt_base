Radiation stress under finite observer boosts and response-preserving energy moments

                                                Anonymous research draft
                                                 (Dated: 27 October 2026)
                A low temperature quadrupole approximates radiation stress only within its amplitude and spec-
             tral assumptions. We compare exact bolometric fourth-power conversion with a linear temperature-
             to-moment adapter on the same six positive synthetic skies and the same correlated errors. Both
             adapters use the same finite-boost stress residual and invariant rest-pressure anisotropy. Their
             physical-target coverage and interval widths differ even when their method-target calibration is
             similar. Separately, energy moments of angular ranks zero, two and four preserve the complete
             five-dimensional radiative shear-response operator. In a retained compression example, rank-four
             response agreement reaches numerical precision while a positive angular entropy residual remains.
             These are conditional full-sky radiation methods; applying them to measured channels requires
             calibrated intensity, spectral completion and a measured joint error law. The accompanying finite-
             bandpass foreground analysis addresses a different inverse problem and does not supply those inputs.

                        II.   CONDITIONAL CALIBRATION, COVERAGE AND WIDTHS

   Each case uses 8,191 calibration skies and 4,096 disjoint test skies. Calibration is pointwise around the known
noiseless response of each method. For D, the confidence-region radius is the 7,783rd ordered calibration norm error,
producing a five-dimensional ball. For Aγ , the interval subtracts the 7,988th and 204th ordered calibration errors
and intersects the result with [0, ∞). An upper endpoint below zero produces an empty interval and is counted as
noncoverage. The exact physical target and the noiseless method target are assessed separately; no bias correction is
fitted to force them to coincide.
   For the pure-boost case at b = 0.00123, the exact and linear physical-target D counts are 3,872/4,096 and
3,035/4,096, while the invariant-anisotropy counts are 3,914/4,096 and 1,545/4,096. Their D ball diameters are
nearly equal, 3.390 × 10−6 and 3.381 × 10−6 . Thus similar widths can conceal different target bias. The linear
method-target coverage remains near its nominal level across the cases even where its physical-target coverage fails.
