# Primary bibliography and minimal public-data intake

Prepared 2026-10-23 from `agent_selection.md`, `agent_observables.md`, current `THEOREMS.md`, and retained source reports. This is a deduplicated list of **39 primary papers**, including the four 2026 cosmography/anisotropy sources. Targeted metadata checks resolved a few titles and official data entry points; no new broad literature search or raw-data acquisition was performed. Publication year and preprint year are distinguished where material.

## Interpretation contract

`inputs/MODEL_TO_DATA.md` and `THEOREMS.md` T7 control the full research object. The tuple is functional/set/path-valued: **x** is sample × functional with tensor rank/parity; **Q** is a matching anchor gauge/margin; **F** separates directional support, sample pushforward, and occupancy under a declared measure; **Pi** is an exceedance surface under an explicit source/posterior/null law; **G_F** is a structured functional depth path retaining tensors, windows, shared covariance, and anchor/support information. Pi is not generically a p-value. Undefined, unavailable, empty, unbounded, and unresolved are distinct states.

The earlier scalar `x_C`, shear-sector `Q=x_C/U_sigma`, sign-clean fraction, and floored bin-summary ratio are eligible projections only. They do not replace T7. In particular, a ratio of production bin `f_value` summaries is not an expectation of samplewise ratios. Literature supports the stated ingredients and conditional comparisons; it does not certify an absent joint source law, tensor response, coverage event, or physical family atlas. The mathematical applications in this package assert no new methodological priority.

## Selection, transport, and honest inference

**01. Mandel, I.; Farr, W. M.; Gair, J. R. (2019). “Extracting distribution parameters from multiple uncertain observations with selection biases.”** [DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stz896](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stz896); [arXiv:1809.02063](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02063). **Supports:** selected-population likelihood normalization and measurement/selection dependence. **Limit:** the detection rule, response law, and normalization must be specified; conditioning does not make unknown quality cuts ignorable.

**02. Fithian, W.; Sun, D.; Taylor, J. (2014; revised 2017). “Optimal Inference After Model Selection.”** [arXiv:1410.2597](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.2597). **Supports:** selective error control conditional on the selection event, including exponential-family conditioning. **Limit:** requires the relevant selection event and statistical model; not a reconstruction of undocumented catalogue selection.

**03. Aronow, P. M.; Lee, D. K. K. (2013). “Interval estimation of population means under unknown but bounded probabilities of sample selection.”** [DOI: 10.1093/biomet/ass064](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/ass064). **Supports:** sensitivity intervals from sharp bounds on a Hájek mean with bounded inclusion probabilities. **Limit:** the probability range and sampling framework are assumptions; statistical weights are not physical source masses.

**04. Zhao, Q.; Small, D. S.; Bhattacharya, B. B. (2019). “Sensitivity Analysis for Inverse Probability Weighting Estimators via the Percentile Bootstrap.”** [DOI: 10.1111/rssb.12327](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12327). **Supports:** marginal-odds sensitivity and optimization-based asymptotic confidence intervals. **Limit:** positivity, model/regularity conditions, and justified sensitivity parameters remain necessary; bootstrap is not source-law validation.

**05. Dorn, J.; Guo, K. (online 2022). “Sharp Sensitivity Analysis for Inverse Propensity Weighting via Quantile Balancing.”** [DOI: 10.1080/01621459.2022.2069572](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2022.2069572); [arXiv:2102.04543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04543). **Supports:** normalization/quantile constraints yield sharp marginal-sensitivity bounds. **Limit:** identification and inferential assumptions differ; unobserved support and optical-to-kinematic kernels are not learned by reweighting.

**06. Tudball, M. J.; Hughes, R. A.; Tilling, K.; Bowden, J.; Zhao, Q. (2023; online 2022). “Sample-constrained partial identification with application to selection bias.”** [DOI: 10.1093/biomet/asac042](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asac042). **Supports:** tighten sensitivity sets with population information while relaxing estimated constraints for coverage. **Limit:** Theorem 1 is asymptotic under its assumptions 1–6; estimated moments cannot be imposed as exact population facts.

**07. Imbens, G. W.; Manski, C. F. (2004). “Confidence Intervals for Partially Identified Parameters.”** [DOI: 10.1111/j.1468-0262.2004.00555.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2004.00555.x). **Supports:** distinguish true-parameter coverage from simultaneous identified-set coverage; bounded missing-outcome examples. **Limit:** endpoint estimation and uniformity near point identification require explicit treatment.

**08. Berger, R. L.; Boos, D. D. (1994). “P Values Maximized Over a Confidence Set for the Nuisance Parameter.”** [DOI: 10.1080/01621459.1994.10476836](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476836). **Supports:** valid nuisance-envelope p-value `min(1, sup_C p_eta + beta)`. **Limit:** needs valid fixed-nuisance p-values, nuisance-set coverage, and a genuine upper bound for the supremum; scanning invalid models does not help.

**09. Josey, K. P.; Berkowitz, S. A.; Ghosh, D.; Raghavan, S. (2021). “Transporting Experimental Results with Entropy Balancing.”** [DOI: 10.1002/sim.9031](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.9031); [primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8487904/). **Supports:** outcome/selection-model double robustness and moment balancing for transport. **Limit:** exchangeability and overlap remain; treatment-trial assumptions do not automatically apply to astronomical tracers.

**10. Chernozhukov, V.; Chetverikov, D.; Demirer, M.; Duflo, E.; Hansen, C.; Newey, W.; Robins, J. (2018). “Double/debiased machine learning for treatment and structural parameters.”** [DOI: 10.1111/ectj.12097](https://doi.org/10.1111/ectj.12097). **Supports:** Neyman-orthogonal scores, cross-fitting, and nuisance-product remainder control. **Limit:** sampling units, moments, overlap, rates, and dependence must be qualified; orthogonality does not remove physical response bias.

**11. Efron, B.; Petrosian, V. (1992). “A simple test of independence for truncated data with applications to redshift surveys.”** [DOI: 10.1086/171931](https://doi.org/10.1086/171931). **Supports:** truncated-data conditional/rank tests without specifying the entire bivariate density. **Limit:** the truncation region and independence null must be appropriate; unknown multilayer targeting is not a known flux threshold.

**12. White, H. (1982). “Maximum Likelihood Estimation of Misspecified Models.”** [DOI: 10.2307/1912526](https://doi.org/10.2307/1912526). **Supports:** quasi-likelihood limits and misspecification-robust inference. **Limit:** the limiting parameter may differ from the physical target; robust covariance cannot validate a rejected residual distribution.

**13. Armstrong, T. B.; Kolesár, M. (2021). “Sensitivity Analysis using Approximate Moment Condition Models.”** [DOI: 10.3982/QE1609](https://doi.org/10.3982/QE1609); [arXiv:1808.07387](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.07387). **Supports:** bias-aware inference and weighting under bounded moment misspecification. **Limit:** the bias set and local asymptotic experiment are substantive assumptions; an unbounded common-mode foreground yields no finite certificate.

## CMB processing, anisotropy, and tensor morphology

**14. Planck Collaboration (2020). “Planck 2018 results. IV. Diffuse component separation.”** [DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/201833881](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833881); [arXiv:1807.06208](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06208). **Supports:** split products, masks, component propagation, and noise/systematic diagnostic distinctions. **Limit:** marginal simulation families are not automatically a joint FULL/HM/OE experiment; fixed weights and data/simulation discrepancies matter.

**15. Planck Collaboration (2020). “Planck 2018 results. VIII. Gravitational lensing.”** [DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/201833886](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833886). **Supports:** the 2018 release lacks HM FFP10 CMB simulations; FFP9/FFP10 seed reuse does not remove beam/weight differences. **Limit:** this is a precise release-level gap, not a claim that all useful Planck analyses are impossible.

**16. Sheehy, C.; Slosar, A. (2018). “No evidence for dust B-mode decorrelation in Planck data.”** [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevD.97.043522](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.97.043522); [arXiv:1709.09729](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.09729). **Supports:** nominal split cross-spectra can have correlated-noise/systematic biases. **Limit:** cross-noise assumptions and sky-patch dependence must be checked for the actual statistic.

**17. Umiltà, C.; Cardoso, J.-F.; Benabed, K. (2019). “The CMB angular power spectrum via component separation.”** [DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/201833758](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833758); [arXiv:1807.00830](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00830). **Supports:** cross-only SMICA avoids auto-noise bias under its assumptions. **Limit:** foreground residual modelling and cross-noise treatment still affect inference; cross-only does not remove common contamination.

**18. Elsner, F.; Leistedt, B.; Peiris, H. V. (2017). “Unbiased pseudo-Cℓ power spectrum estimation with mode projection.”** [DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stw2752](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stw2752); [arXiv:1609.03577](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03577). **Supports:** analytical correction of template-projection bias. **Limit:** projection removes aligned signal information and covers only the specified nuisance span and observation operator.

**19. Planck Collaboration (2014). “Planck 2013 results. XXVII. Doppler boosting of the CMB.”** [DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/201321556](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201321556); [arXiv:1303.5087](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.5087). **Supports:** local observer boost from aberration/modulation, with frequency-dependent intensity-to-temperature response. **Limit:** a radiation-frame velocity at one event is not a global matter-tilt history.

**20. Land, K.; Magueijo, J. (2005). “Multipole invariants and non-Gaussianity.”** [DOI: 10.1111/j.1745-3933.2005.00065.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3933.2005.00065.x). **Supports:** separating rotational invariants, power, and axis information in CMB multipoles; context for T3. **Limit:** the package's orbit-distance and degenerate-stratum calculations are explicit mathematical applications, not a new literature novelty claim or a physical-history identification theorem.

**21. Saadeh, D.; Feeney, S. M.; Pontzen, A.; Peiris, H. V.; McEwen, J. D. (2016). “A framework for testing isotropy with the cosmic microwave background.”** [DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stw1731](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stw1731); [arXiv:1604.01024](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01024). **Supports:** physical Bianchi shear-mode evolution and the corresponding CMB transfer framework. **Limit:** family, initial conditions, mode histories, and normalization are part of the result; not a substitute for the missing HTT all-m atlas.

**22. Saadeh, D.; Feeney, S. M.; Pontzen, A.; Peiris, H. V.; McEwen, J. D. (2016). “How Isotropic is the Universe?”** [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.131302](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.131302). **Supports:** empirical anisotropic-expansion constraints within the declared CMB/Bianchi analysis. **Limit:** published limits depend on transfer, model, and priors; they are not HTT joint source draws or universal shear/vorticity limits.

## Astrometry, frames, sources, and angular drift

**23. Mignard, F.; Klioner, S. (2012). “Analysis of astrometric catalogues with vector spherical harmonics.”** [DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/201219927](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201219927); [arXiv:1207.0025](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0025). **Supports:** E/B vector harmonics and rotation/glide decomposition. **Limit:** orthogonality on the sphere does not ensure orthogonality in a masked, weighted catalogue; use its actual design Gram matrix.

**24. Gaia Collaboration (2022). “Gaia Early Data Release 3. The celestial reference frame (Gaia-CRF3).”** [DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/202243483](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202243483); [arXiv:2204.12574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12574). **Supports:** reference-frame construction, spatial proper-motion systematics, and optical/radio offsets. **Limit:** per-source 2×2 covariance blocks do not supply the full sky-field covariance or a matter-following source law.

**25. Gaia Collaboration (2021). “Gaia Early Data Release 3. Acceleration of the Solar System from Gaia astrometry.”** [DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/202039734](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202039734). **Supports:** a measured acceleration/glide E1 field under a specified frame/source analysis. **Limit:** this does not uniquely separate arbitrary local and cosmological E1 contributions.

**26. Petrov, L.; Kovalev, Y. Y. (2017). “Observational consequences of optical band milliarcsecond-scale structure in active galactic nuclei discovered by Gaia.”** [DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stx1747](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stx1747); [arXiv:1704.07365](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.07365). **Supports:** optical structure and variability can move the photocentre differently from VLBI compact structure. **Limit:** better astrometric precision alone does not make the centroid a dust/matter tracer.

**27. Petrov, L.; Kovalev, Y. Y.; Plavin, A. V. (2019; online 2018). “A quantitative analysis of systematic differences in the positions and proper motions of Gaia DR2 with respect to VLBI.”** [DOI: 10.1093/mnras/sty2807](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/sty2807); [NASA author reprint](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190028777). **Supports:** jet-aligned offsets/proper motions and source-dependent photocentre effects. **Limit:** the population comparison is not a calibrated joint optical/radio response for each current source.

**28. Quercellini, C., et al. (2009). “Cosmic parallax as a probe of late-time anisotropic expansion.”** [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevD.80.063527](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.80.063527); [arXiv:0905.4853](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.4853). **Supports:** model-dependent angular-drift predictions/forecasts. **Limit:** its cosmological family and observer/source assumptions must accompany any use; it supplies no HTT-specific transfer atlas.

**29. Fontanini, M.; West, E. J.; Trodden, M. (2009). “Can cosmic parallax distinguish between anisotropic cosmologies?”** [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevD.80.123515](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.80.123515). **Supports:** comparing parallax responses across specified anisotropic models. **Limit:** forecast distinguishability is conditional on models and measurement assumptions, not a universal response inversion.

**30. Marcori, O. H.; Pitrou, C.; Uzan, J.-P.; Pereira, T. S. (2018). “Direction and redshift drifts for general observers and their applications in cosmology.”** [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevD.98.023517](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.98.023517); [arXiv:1805.12121](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12121). **Supports:** decomposition into parallax/aberration drifts and explicit FLRW/Bianchi-I applications; source of the restricted K03 analytic specialization. **Limit:** that specialization retains source, depth, velocity-order, and remainder assumptions; formal rank does not establish the small-velocity domain.

**31. Truebenbach, A. E.; Darling, J. (2017). “The VLBA Extragalactic Proper Motion Catalog and a Measurement of the Secular Aberration Drift.”** [arXiv:1710.02099](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02099). **Supports:** radio source proper-motion slopes, secular-aberration analysis, and possible attenuation by no-net-rotation constraints. **Limit:** radio processing and source histories require their own response/covariance; no generic optical/radio B1 transfer is implied.

**32. Storey-Fisher, K.; Hogg, D. W.; Rix, H.-W.; Eilers, A.-C.; Fabbian, G.; Blanton, M.; Alonso, D. (2024). “Quaia, the Gaia-unWISE Quasar Catalog: An All-Sky Spectroscopic Quasar Sample.”** [DOI: 10.3847/1538-4357/ad1328](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ad1328); [arXiv:2306.17749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17749). **Supports:** the catalogue, redshift estimates, cuts, and angular selection products relevant to the retained 755,850-row sample. **Limit:** relative angular selection maps are not absolute individual inclusion probabilities or a physical photocentre-motion/depth likelihood.

## Covariant cosmography and the 2026 comparisons

**33. Heinesen, A. (2021). “Multipole decomposition of the general luminosity-distance Hubble law.”** [DOI: 10.1088/1475-7516/2021/05/008](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2021/05/008); [arXiv:2010.06534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06534). **Supports:** general low-redshift covariant coefficients without fixing a metric family. **Limit:** smooth congruence, regular optical vertex, local redshift invertibility, and finite-order remainder matter; optical shear and timelike-congruence shear are different tensors.

**34. Maartens, R., et al. (2024). “Covariant cosmography: observer-dependence of the Hubble parameter.”** [DOI: 10.1088/1475-7516/2024/09/070](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2024/09/070); [arXiv:2312.09875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09875). **Supports:** local observer boost, Hubble dipole/quadrupole relations, and correct distance transformation in geodesic dust. **Limit:** a moving observer is not a global boosted congruence; intrinsic acceleration changes the dipole interpretation beyond dust.

**35. Heinesen, A.; Korzyński, M. (2024). “Exploring geometrical information in cosmic drift signals.”** [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevD.110.043525](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.110.043525); [arXiv:2406.06167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06167). **Supports:** combining position/redshift drift and distance information to constrain geometry. **Limit:** finite-distance drifts involve regional kinematic/tidal information; they are not simply one local velocity-gradient tensor.

**36. Kalbouneh, B., et al. (2026). “The anisotropic expansion rate of the local Universe and its covariant cosmographic interpretation.”** [DOI: 10.1088/1475-7516/2026/03/084](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2026/03/084); [arXiv:2510.02510v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02510v2). **Supports:** observed CF4/Pantheon+ directional multipoles and a covariant interpretation with angular/flux/group treatment. **Limit:** finite-bin multipoles and higher cosmographic coefficients are not automatically point-local shear or global anisotropic histories.

**37. Stiskalek, R.; Desmond, H.; Lavaux, G. (2026). “No evidence for local H0 anisotropy from Tully–Fisher or supernova distances.”** [DOI: 10.1093/mnras/staf2048](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/staf2048); [arXiv:2509.14997v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14997v2). **Supports:** joint calibration/distance/velocity forward modelling and a radial velocity-dipole interpretation of observed directional features. **Limit:** its conclusion concerns that larger nuisance model and sample/range; it does not say raw dipoles are absent or settle every alternative model.

**38. Bendtsen, J. B.; Heinesen, A.; Koksbang, S. M. (2026 preprint). “Cosmography for a General Spacetime Centred at Arbitrary Redshift.”** [arXiv:2608.07008v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07008v1). **Supports:** arbitrary-redshift expansions and the distinction between finite-range effective coefficients and point-local geometry. **Limit:** the examples do not supply an HTT-specific finite-redshift remainder; treat this as a preprint, not an established empirical calibration.

**39. Clarkson, C.; Maartens, R. (2026 preprint). “Beyond dust in local cosmography: covariant kinetic theory.”** [arXiv:2610.04284v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04284v1). **Supports:** exact kinetic momentum/acceleration relations, intrinsic Hubble dipoles beyond geodesic dust, and acceleration-curl effects; equations 18, 52, 56–58 underpin T1. **Limit:** exact constraints and truncated evolution closures are different; no quasar photocentre law or HTT provider calibration follows. T1's positivity/perturbation bounds are explicit conditional applications, not a novelty claim.
