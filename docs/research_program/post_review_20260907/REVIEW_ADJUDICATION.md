## Verdict on the reviews

The central criticism is justified: an internally consistent pedagogical exposition is not yet a distinctive, quantitatively useful observational method. The earlier artifact acceptance established delivery and bounded source/build correspondence, not novelty, all-proof formal verification or a published research conclusion. A strong successor paper must identify an estimand, an observable information gain, a calibrated uncertainty and a comparison with existing complete morphology representations. That does not make the existing conditional mathematics worthless or overturn its document acceptance.

The most useful research centre is **the difference between algebraic degeneracy and quantitative identifiability under a physically declared nuisance class, with genuinely additional redshift/frame information**. The old universal 'no-go' wording should not become the project's only output. Neither should a prior-driven posterior be relabelled model-independent identification.

## Literature corrections and research role

Primary sources consulted include:

- Chluba and Ravenni, *The boost operator: properties, computation, and applications*, MNRAS 548 (2026), stag698, DOI `10.1093/mnras/stag698`; initial preprint `arXiv:2505.02080`. The publisher version was actually read, including the conventions and its restricted lowest-multipole dipole-degeneracy argument. The initial arXiv record alone is not the 2026 revised journal source.
- Land and Magueijo, *The Multipole Vectors of WMAP, and their frames and invariants*, `astro-ph/0502574`, MNRAS 362 (2005), 838–846. This identifier is theirs, not a Copi et al. paper. The published frame/invariant representation must be part of the comparator landscape.
- Ferreira and Quartin, PRL 127 (2021), 101301, `arXiv:2011.08385`, and their methodology paper `arXiv:2107.10846`. They distinguish aberration, Doppler modulation and the temperature dipole, including the spurious intensity-to-temperature term. Their results show why the low-QO LS variance cannot be advertised as a no-go for the entire CMB.
- Deutsch et al., *Reconstruction of the remote dipole and quadrupole fields from the kinetic Sunyaev Zel'dovich and polarized Sunyaev Zel'dovich effects*, PRD 98, 123501, `arXiv:1707.08129`. Its remote-field interpretation is a windowed scattering response with electron/optical-depth structure, not direct observation of every fundamental observer.
- Graziani et al., `arXiv:1901.01818`, explicitly forward-model distances and the velocity/density field; Sorce et al., `arXiv:2309.03945`, address lognormal and Malmquist biases. These motivate checking the actual CF4 data product and its selection model before adopting a Gaussian radial-velocity likelihood.

The citations provide model and comparator context. None is claimed to prove the project-specific soft-mask, sharper-envelope or data-contract conclusions merely by being listed. The exact physical and statistical contracts still have to be frozen before the final Codex data campaign.
