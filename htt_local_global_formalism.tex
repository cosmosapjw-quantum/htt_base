\begin{abstract}
\noindent This note is a self-contained, first-principles description of how the
current code separates a \emph{local kinematic boost} (the observer's peculiar
motion, an $\ell=1$ Doppler/aberration effect) from a \emph{global tilt} (a
cosmological bulk flow, the $\Otilt$ sector of the anisotropy comparator
$g=(\Sig,\Wsq,\Otilt,\Ok)$). It states the observables, the leading-order
response map that the pipeline actually uses, and --- crucially --- the
second-order ($\beta^2$) coupling by which a boost leaks power into the
quadrupole and could masquerade as a shear $\Sig$. It then explains the five
concrete safeguards already in the code, shows why the comparator covariance is
\emph{not} diagonal beyond leading order, and gives the precise form of the two
formalism upgrades (a kinematic projection operator and a coupled Fisher matrix)
that would deproject the leakage. The document is written so that the ``Leaky
Universe'' toy test (\S\ref{sec:toy}) can be answered with justification. All
content is diagnostic-only: no Bianchi family is identified, no geometry is
detected, and no shear amplitude is reported as measured.
\end{abstract}

% ======================================================================
\section{The diagnostic vector and its two velocity-like sectors}
\label{sec:vector}

The anisotropy comparator is the graded vector
\begin{equation}
  g=(\Sig,\ \Wsq,\ \Otilt,\ \Ok)\in\mathbb{R}^4 ,
\end{equation}
carrying the shear, vorticity, tilt, and anisotropic-curvature sectors
(\pathx{htt/obsstat/egs3_graded_comparator.py}). Two of these are directly
velocity-like and are the subject of this note:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Otilt$ --- the \textbf{global tilt}: the peculiar velocity of the matter
frame relative to the CMB frame, i.e.\ a cosmological \emph{bulk flow}. It is a
genuine large-scale degree of freedom.
\item the \textbf{local boost} $\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf v_{\mathrm{obs}}/c$ ---
the \emph{observer's} own peculiar motion. This is not a sector of $g$; it is a
frame effect. Its leading imprint is the CMB kinematic dipole ($\ell=1$).
\end{itemize}
The central difficulty is that both are ``a velocity in a direction $\vhat$'', so
their sky imprints overlap. The pipeline's job is to keep them distinct.

\section{Observables and the leading-order response map}
\label{sec:response}

The low-$\ell$ CMB channel is the set of temperature multipole coefficients
$\alm$ for $\ell\ge 2$; the peculiar-velocity channel is the CF4 radial
velocity field. The code encodes the \emph{leading-order} sensitivity of each
observable channel to each sector as a $\{0,1\}$ support pattern $D$
(\pathx{egs3_graded_comparator.py:_RESPONSE_SUPPORT}), Table~\ref{tab:D}.

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\small
\caption{The leading-EGS-order response map $D$. Entry $1$: the channel responds
to that sector at leading order; $0$: structurally blind.}
\label{tab:D}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
channel $\backslash$ sector & $\Sig$ & $\Wsq$ & $\Otilt$ & $\Ok$ \\
\midrule
CMB quadrupole ($\ell{=}2$)   & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
CMB dipole ($\ell{=}1$)       & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
radial-velocity dipole        & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

At this (leading, EGS) order the map is \emph{block-diagonal in the two velocity
sectors}: the quadrupole responds to $\Sig$ only, and both dipole channels
respond to $\Otilt$ only. The reachable subspace is rank~2
($\{\Sig,\Otilt\}$); $\Wsq$ and $\Ok$ are the two null sectors. This is exactly
the separation the toy test interrogates: is the ``quadrupole $=$ shear''
assumption safe once the observer moves?

\paragraph{Honest scope of $D$.} The code labels $D$ as a documented
\emph{leading-EGS-order} support pattern, and states in-module that ``the exact
response amplitudes need the covariant transfer''. The block-diagonal structure
of Table~\ref{tab:D} is therefore an $O(\beta^1)$ statement, not an exact one. The
next section makes the $O(\beta^2)$ correction explicit.

\section{The leakage: a boost sources a quadrupole}
\label{sec:leak}

A boost of the observer by $\boldsymbol\beta$ transforms a blackbody monopole
$T_0$. With $\mu=\nhat\!\cdot\!\vhat$ the Doppler/aberration expansion of the
observed temperature is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Delta T(\nhat)}{T_0}
  = \beta\,\mu \;+\; \beta^2\!\left(\mu^2-\tfrac13\right)\cdot\mathcal{O}(1)
    \;+\; O(\beta^3).
  \label{eq:boost}
\end{equation}
The first term is the dipole ($\ell=1$, order $\beta$). The second term contains
a \textbf{kinematic quadrupole}, because $\mu^2=\tfrac23 P_2(\mu)+\tfrac13$: a
boost necessarily deposits an $\ell=2$ signal of amplitude
\begin{equation}
  a_{2m}^{\mathrm{kin}}\ \sim\ \beta^2\,T_0 ,
  \qquad
  C_2^{\mathrm{kin}}\ \sim\ (\beta^2 T_0)^2 ,
\end{equation}
aligned with the boost axis $\vhat$. Numerically, for $\beta\simeq10^{-3}$
(a $300\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ motion) one has $\beta^2\simeq10^{-6}$ and
$\beta^2 T_0\simeq$ a few $\mu$K, i.e.\ $C_2^{\mathrm{kin}}$ of order a few
$\mu$K$^2$ --- small against the observed $C_2\sim\mathcal{O}(10^2)\,\mu$K$^2$,
but \emph{nonzero, sign-definite, and axis-aligned}.

\paragraph{Why this breaks the diagonal.} Because the same velocity that sets the
dipole (hence $\Otilt$) also sets this quadrupole, the true response map has an
extra column entry that Table~\ref{tab:D} omits:
\begin{equation}
  D_{\text{quad},\,\Otilt}\ \propto\ \beta^2\ \neq\ 0 .
  \label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
The exact $D$ is therefore \emph{not} block-diagonal, and the comparator
covariance / Fisher matrix inherits a nonzero off-diagonal
$F_{\Sig,\Otilt}$. A naive estimator that reads all quadrupole power as $\Sig$
will report a false positive $\Sig>0$ in a universe whose only anisotropy is a
bulk flow. \emph{This is the correct and important content of the toy test.}

\section{How the current pipeline keeps the two apart}
\label{sec:safeguards}

The code does not yet deproject \eqref{eq:coupling} inside the low-$\ell$
statistics (there is no kinematic-quadrupole subtraction in the OBSSTAT
low-$\ell$ path; the $O(\beta)$ Challinor aberration kernel in
\pathx{htt/bass/hierarchy/boost_kernel.py} lives on the Boltzmann source side,
not here). Instead, the separation is enforced by five structural safeguards, so
that the leakage cannot become a false \emph{measurement}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The dipole is excluded ($\ell_{\min}=2$).} All morphology
statistics are computed on $\ell\in[2,\ell_{\max}]$
(\pathx{make_lowell_morphology_real_map.py:ELL_MIN=2}). The dominant
$O(\beta)$ boost imprint never enters the statistics directly; only the much
smaller $O(\beta^2)$ quadrupole can leak.
\item \textbf{No shear amplitude is ever reported as measured.} $\Sig$ is held at
\code{partial} (K1 is a look-elsewhere descriptor under a $\Lambda$CDM null; see
\pathx{pr08_006_joint_artifact.json}). The pipeline returns rank and
look-elsewhere $p$-values, not a shear amplitude, so a $\beta^2$ leak cannot be
promoted into a ``detected shear''. The blind/partial sectors \emph{fail closed};
they are never set from the quadrupole alone.
\item \textbf{A dedicated boost-alignment statistic.} One of the six registered
statistics is \pathx{axis_to_cmb_dipole_deg}: the angle between the low-$\ell$
preferred axis and the \emph{CMB kinematic dipole apex}. A boost-induced
quadrupole is aligned with $\vhat$ by construction, so it shows up precisely as
an anomalously small axis-to-apex angle. The pipeline therefore \emph{measures
the very signature} the leakage would produce, and calibrates it against the null
--- rather than silently absorbing it into $\Sig$.
\item \textbf{The tilt is anchored by an independent channel.} $\Otilt$ is
measured from the CF4 radial peculiar velocities (K5 bulk flow,
$|\mathbf B|\simeq341\pm102\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$), \emph{not} from the CMB
quadrupole. Because the tilt is fixed by velocity data, the CMB quadrupole is not
asked to carry it; the observational degeneracy of \eqref{eq:coupling} is broken
by using two different data sets for the two sectors.
\item \textbf{A local/global claim firewall.} Local (depth-resolved) and global
(bulk) statements are kept structurally distinct: the MIO depth-gap diagnostic
$G_F$ explicitly forbids the string ``global tilt'' in its metadata
(\pathx{htt/mio/formalism/isotropy_gap.py:_FORBIDDEN_G_METADATA_TERMS}), and
$G_F$ is documented as a depth-local bookkeeping quantity, not evidence for a
global tilt. A local boost and a global tilt cannot be conflated through the
diagnostic layer.
\end{enumerate}

The E2E null (the matched Planck simulation ensemble; see the K1 download guide)
is the calibration that turns safeguard~3 quantitative: any residual frame /
boost quadrupole present in the observed map but absent from the simulations
raises the look-elsewhere score, which is exactly where such a systematic should
appear.

\section{The comparator covariance is not diagonal}
\label{sec:fisher}

Let the data be $d$ with covariance $C$ and model $d=D\,g$ (locally linear
response). The Fisher information on $g$ is
\begin{equation}
  F \;=\; D^{\mathsf T} C^{-1} D ,
  \qquad
  \Cov(\hat g)\ \gtrsim\ F^{-1}.
\end{equation}
With the exact $D$ carrying the $\beta^2$ entry \eqref{eq:coupling}, the block
between the two velocity-like sectors is
\begin{equation}
  F_{\{\Sig,\Otilt\}}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    F_{\Sig\Sig} & F_{\Sig\Otilt}\\[2pt]
    F_{\Sig\Otilt} & F_{\Otilt\Otilt}
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  F_{\Sig\Otilt}\ \propto\ \beta^2\ \neq 0 .
  \label{eq:Fblock}
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal is the analytic statement of the degeneracy: at fixed data, an
increase in the inferred $\Otilt$ can be partly absorbed by a decrease in $\Sig$
along the eigenvector of \eqref{eq:Fblock} with the smaller eigenvalue. The
leading-order code map Table~\ref{tab:D} sets $F_{\Sig\Otilt}=0$ \emph{by
construction}; that is precisely the approximation the toy test stresses, and the
reason the pipeline relies on the structural safeguards of \S\ref{sec:safeguards}
rather than on the diagonal assumption.

\section{The two formalism upgrades, made precise}
\label{sec:tweaks}

Both remedies proposed in the toy test are correct and slot directly onto the
existing response-design / identifiability machinery.

\subsection{Upgrade 1 --- a kinematic projection operator}
Define the deprojected (orthogonalised) shear estimator
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\Sig}\ =\ \Sig \;-\; \alpha\,(\Otilt)^2 ,
  \label{eq:proj}
\end{equation}
which subtracts the boost-sourced quadrupole \emph{before} $\Sig$ is read off.
The coefficient $\alpha$ is fixed analytically by \eqref{eq:boost}: since the
kinematic quadrupole scales as $\beta^2$ and $\Otilt\propto\beta$ (a tilt is
linear in the velocity), $(\Otilt)^2\propto\beta^2$ matches the leakage exactly,
and $\alpha$ is the ratio of the boost-quadrupole response to the shear
quadrupole response,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha
  =\frac{\partial\,C_2^{\mathrm{kin}}/\partial(\Otilt)^2}
        {\partial\,C_2^{\mathrm{shear}}/\partial\Sig}\Bigg|_{\text{leading order}} .
\end{equation}
Operationally this is a projection $\mathcal P$ that removes, from the
quadrupole, the component along the boost-quadrupole template
$Y_{2m}(\vhat)$ (a $\vhat$-aligned $\ell=2$ pattern), leaving the residual to
$\Sig$. In the code this is the natural extension of the response design $D$:
add the $\beta^2$ row of \eqref{eq:coupling} and project it out, so that
$\widetilde{\Sig}\to 0$ in a pure-bulk-flow sky.

\subsection{Upgrade 2 --- a coupled Fisher prior}
Rather than subtracting, carry the coupling \eqref{eq:Fblock} into the sampler as
a prior. Compute $F_{\Sig\Otilt}\propto\beta^2$ analytically and hand the full
$2\times2$ block to the MCMC/MCTS as the parameter-space metric. The chain then
explores along the degeneracy eigenvector rather than treating $\Sig$ and
$\Otilt$ as independent axes, and a bulk-flow-only sky produces a posterior that
is elongated along ``trade $\Sig$ against $\Otilt$'' with $\Sig$ consistent with
zero, instead of a spurious $\Sig>0$ point estimate. This is the
covariance-aware version of the identifiability rank already computed in
\pathx{egs3_graded_comparator.py:identifiable_rank}: the off-diagonal reduces
the \emph{effective} rank in the $\{\Sig,\Otilt\}$ block whenever $\beta$ is
large enough that the two responses overlap.

\paragraph{Relation between the two.} Upgrade~1 is a point-estimate
deprojection; upgrade~2 is its Bayesian dual. They agree at leading order: the
projection \eqref{eq:proj} is the maximum-likelihood solution of the coupled
Fisher block \eqref{eq:Fblock} after marginalising $\Otilt$.

\section{The ``Leaky Universe'' test, answered}
\label{sec:toy}

\emph{Setup: a spatially flat sky ($\Ok=0$) with no shear ($\Sig=0$) and a single
injected bulk flow $\Otilt$ of $300\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$; Planck-level
temperature noise; CF4-level (30\%) distance errors; severe cosmic variance at
$\ell\le30$.}

\begin{description}
\item[Q1. Does a naive pipeline report a false $\Sig>0$?] \textbf{Yes} --- a naive
estimator that equates all quadrupole power with shear will pick up the
$\beta^2$ kinematic quadrupole of \eqref{eq:boost} and report $\Sig>0$. This is a
real effect, correctly identified. It is the reason the diagonal map
Table~\ref{tab:D} is labelled leading-order-only.
\item[Q2. Does the \emph{current} pipeline report a false detection?] \textbf{No.}
It does not report a shear amplitude at all: $\Sig$ is \code{partial}
(look-elsewhere only), so there is no amplitude to be falsely inflated
(safeguard~2). The $\ell=1$ boost is excluded (safeguard~1). The
boost-aligned quadrupole instead registers on the \pathx{axis_to_cmb_dipole_deg}
statistic (safeguard~3), which is calibrated against the null --- i.e.\ the
pipeline would flag ``low-$\ell$ axis aligned with the dipole apex'', the honest
description of a boost, not ``shear detected''. And the tilt itself is measured
from CF4 velocities (safeguard~4), so the quadrupole is never asked to supply it.
\item[Q3. Is $\Cov(g)$ diagonal?] \textbf{No} --- \eqref{eq:Fblock}:
$F_{\Sig\Otilt}\propto\beta^2\neq0$. The code's leading-order map sets it to zero
\emph{by construction}, which is why the pipeline leans on the structural
safeguards rather than the diagonal assumption. Making the off-diagonal explicit
is upgrade~2.
\item[Q4. Are the two proposed fixes valid?] \textbf{Yes, both.} The kinematic
projection operator \eqref{eq:proj} with the analytic $\alpha$ deprojects the
leakage so that $\widetilde{\Sig}\to0$ in a pure-bulk-flow sky; the coupled
Fisher prior \eqref{eq:Fblock} makes the sampler aware of the $\Sig$--$\Otilt$
degeneracy. They are point-estimate and Bayesian versions of the same
deprojection and attach to the existing response-design and identifiability code.
\end{description}

\section{Claim envelope}
This note is diagnostic-only. $\Otilt$ is a model-independent kinematic
descriptor (a bulk flow), not evidence of anisotropic geometry; $\Sig$ is not
reported as a measured amplitude; the local boost and the global tilt are kept
distinct by construction. Nothing here identifies a Bianchi family, detects a
geometry, validates a native low-$\ell$ solver, or promotes any scalar into a
model-selection statement. The kinematic-quadrupole deprojection of
\S\ref{sec:tweaks} is the recommended formalism upgrade; until it is in the
low-$\ell$ statistics, the separation is carried by the five structural
safeguards of \S\ref{sec:safeguards}, and $\Sig$ stays \code{partial}.
